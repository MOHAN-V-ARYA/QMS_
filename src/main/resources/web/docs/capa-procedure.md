# Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA) Procedure

**Document ID:** LDMS-CAPA-008 · **Owner:** Quality Lead

## 1. Trigger and containment
Open a CAPA for recurring or systemic defects, critical/high-risk nonconformance, escaped defect, failed control, audit finding, or material customer complaint. Assign a unique ID, source, affected requirement/release/interface, accountable owner, severity, and due date. Contain immediate customer/data/operational risk first; record containment and escalation.

## 2. Root cause
Preserve evidence, reproduce the issue, identify affected scope and recurrence, then distinguish symptom from cause. Use causal analysis (timeline, cause-and-effect, or iterative why analysis) with cross-functional input. Consider requirement ambiguity, interface assumptions, implementation, review/test gaps, configuration, data, and monitoring. Record evidence, rejected hypotheses, and residual risk; do not state “human error” without a process cause.

## 3. Correct and prevent
Define separate correction (fix the observed issue) and corrective/preventive actions (remove the cause and reduce recurrence). Each action has an owner, due date, affected baseline, validation method, and objective completion evidence. Route requirement/design/code/test changes through the approved change process and CCB where baselines change. Update procedures, contracts, regression coverage, or training only where causal evidence supports it.

## 4. Verify effectiveness
An independent reviewer verifies the action and retest evidence. Define effectiveness criteria before closure, including a suitable observation window, impacted variants/interfaces, recurrence search, and expected outcome. If evidence fails or a recurrence occurs, reopen the CAPA, reassess root cause, and escalate risk. “Action implemented” alone is not proof of effectiveness.
